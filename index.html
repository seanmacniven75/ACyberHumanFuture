This is a model, not a measurement. It shows the shape of the argument rather than a forecast for any particular organisation.

  1. 1What the model does. Five grades hold a starting population of four thousand, shaped as a normal support pyramid. Each simulated year, people join at the bottom, a proportion of each grade is promoted into the grade above, and a proportion of every grade leaves for another employer or retires. Five or ten years are projected.
  2. 2Where automation enters. Promotion is not an entitlement. It depends on having done enough work of the right difficulty to be ready. Automation removes that work from the bottom of the difficulty range upward, so it removes the readiness that feeds each promotion, starting with the first one and working up.
  3. 3The three bands. Up to 60 percent, only the easiest work has gone, so only the first promotion is affected. From 60 to 80 percent, mid-level work goes with it and the middle promotions slow. Above 80 percent, expert-level work goes too and the last promotion slows as well. The boundaries are chosen for the argument and are not measured.
  4. 4Voluntary departure rates are held constant. Nine percent a year at the bottom rising to thirteen percent at Chief Expert, where retirement is added to ordinary turnover. A grade whose promotion route has closed loses people faster, because an engineer who cannot progress goes somewhere that will let them.
  5. 5Headcount follows the work, so redundancy is modelled. Each grade is established against the work that still exists at its level. When the case volume at a grade falls, hiring into it falls with it and any surplus above the new establishment is released over the following years. The "surplus released per year" slider (default 35%) controls how fast that happens: at zero, nobody is forced out and the workforce shrinks only through natural attrition and stagnation departures. The total headcount display shows the sum across all five bands, since a collapsing base is easily missed when watching only the top. The direction matches what Brynjolfsson and colleagues observe in payroll data for the most AI exposed occupations.
  6. 6Hiring experts from outside is not modelled, on purpose. It moves experts between employers and does not create any. When every organisation runs the same automation, the pool being competed over is the one this model is draining.
  7. 7Starting distribution and rates. 1,600, 1,100, 700, 400 and 200 across the five grades, with promotion rates set so that the pyramid holds steady at zero automation. Every number is adjustable and none of them is observed.
  8. 8The scale of the automation pipeline is now visible. SAP's roadmap to 400 AI agents by autumn 2026 across 50 domain-specific assistants is one company's contribution to the automation volume the model here projects. Gartner reports agentic workloads consuming 5 to 30 times more tokens per task than a standard chatbot, because an agent plans, calls tools, checks results and retries. The question is not whether the automation slider moves, but how fast and how far.
The turn

If the foundation for learning evaporates through automation, expertise has to be built on purpose. This turns a future staffing apocalypse into a learning problem.

For a century experts fell out of the pipeline, honed by thousands of cases and problem solving challenges. Remove the work, and we have to compensate deliberately, or risk losing it.

Which is awkward, because deliberate learning is the thing every organisation I have ever engaged with is terrible at.

This slide asserts a mechanism rather than a finding. No source establishes that expertise will fail to appear; the argument is that the route by which it previously appeared is being removed.

  1. 1The evidentiary basis is act one in full: Bainbridge on practice loss, Lee and colleagues on effort shifting from producing to checking, Brynjolfsson and colleagues on entry level employment in AI exposed occupations, and the two simulations on what that does to a career and to a workforce.
  2. 2Not claimed. That expertise cannot be built deliberately, or that it has already failed. Only that it will now require design where it previously required volume.

What most organisations call "learning" is scheduled, packaged and generalised "courses". Virtually always offered out of context, either mandated, or to be executed if or when time allows.

Everyone in this room has clicked through one of these this quarter. It was completed. What did you learn from it?

Observational. This characterises how corporate learning is commonly delivered. It is not drawn from a survey.

  1. 1Scheduled, packaged, generalised are my framing of three recurring failure modes, not established categories.
  2. 2The latency point is the operative one and connects to the 70-20-10 framing: formal learning is the 10, and it is the part least able to arrive at the moment of need.
Act two

Beyond the most basic foundations, the course is seldom the right unit or medium for learning. The majority of learning is on the job, in context and ideally, when actually needed. The 70-20-10 model captures the essence of the problem, while personal relevance heightens learning capabilities further.

Just-in-case
Six hours covering forty things, authored last year, in case one of them is ever needed. This is the model KCS replaced for knowledge. It is still how we do learning.
Just-in-time
Nine minutes of one thing, surfaced when demand for it appears. Sufficient to close the gap, and nothing more. Your own standard, applied to capability.
Training is the last just-in-case function left in the building. People become expert by doing slightly harder things than they can currently manage, next to someone who can already do them. Lave and Wenger called it legitimate peripheral participation.

  3. 3Lave, J. and Wenger, E. (1991). Situated Learning: Legitimate Peripheral Participation. Cambridge University Press. Expertise develops by working at the edge of current ability alongside someone who is already past it.

Act two, the core move

Once objectives are addressable, learning becomes demand-driven and many-to-many, so the organisation can find who needs something and a person can ask for what they need.

Proactive, the organisation asks
The objective broadcasts, and the engineers who would benefit identify themselves. Nobody had to know in advance who they were.
Interactive, the person asks
An engineer, or the AI working beside them, raises a need against a case in progress. The organisation (e.g. via an AI learning agent) answers it. Situational, contextual, and personal.
Learning becomes a market. A similar shift to the one KCS made from knowledge engineering to many-to-many.

Act two

The aggregate of those anonymous signals shows where capability is thin. That is a Learning Loop for people rather than an Evolve Loop for articles.

Solve and Evolve both optimise the knowledge base. Neither has a term for the person. The Learning Loop is that missing term. It runs on signals the architecture already produces, so nothing new has to be collected to feed it.

The aggregate signal claim is a design intent. It has not been measured on a live estate.

Act two, the question

Every one of those flows needs an answer to the same question: who is this for?

Proactive or interactive, broadcast or request, every direction collapses into the same problem. Matching requires knowing something about people. That is exactly the thing we are not allowed to know.
  1. 1The figure of four thousand is the approximate size of the SAP support engineering community I work with. The dots are illustrative.
  2. 2Why asking does not work. Self-assessment of competence is unreliable in both directions, and self-reported need is subject to the same incentive to inflate that makes a self-written profile unusable for matching. Act three, architecture

BUT...and here is the challenge...all of it depends on knowing what each person is able to do. Today, that would require building, storing and maintaining a central register of every employee's skills and weaknesses. An ambition that in the EU would be illegal, and where not, arguably extremely unethical. And yet, there is no personalisation without knowing the person...RIGHT?

Permanent, queryable, and available to whoever later runs a restructuring. It does not matter whether anyone intended to use it that way. It exists: subpoenaed, leaked, hacked or repurposed by the next management team.

Any central storage of capabilities or performance data, is a form of profiling. But any meaningful personalisation, requires profiles. What to do, what to do...

The legal position stated precisely. A central register of employee capability is not flatly illegal in the EU. It would be very difficult to make lawful, and in Germany could not be introduced at all without works council agreement.

  1. 1GDPR. Article 5 on purpose limitation, data minimisation and storage limitation; Article 22 on decisions based solely on automated processing producing legal or similarly significant effects; Article 35 requiring a data protection impact assessment for systematic evaluation of personal aspects. A permanent capability register engages all three.
  2. 2EU AI Act, Annex III. AI systems used in employment for recruitment, promotion, termination, task allocation or evaluation of performance and behaviour are classified as high risk. The Digital Omnibus on AI (Regulation EU 2026/1744, in force 27 July 2026) pushed the Annex III compliance deadline to 2 December 2027. Article 50 transparency obligations and AI Office enforcement powers became active on 2 August 2026. Article 26(7) requires employers to notify workers and their representatives before deploying any high-risk AI system in the workplace.
  3. 3Betriebsverfassungsgesetz paragraph 87(1)(6). Introduction of technical devices capable of monitoring behaviour or performance is subject to works council co-determination. In practice this is decisive in Germany regardless of the data protection analysis.
  4. 4The ethical claim is mine and is separate from the legal one. The argument is about what such a register becomes under a future management team, not about anyone's present intent.

Act three, the mechanism

Now the question from act two has an answer: a Duo can be matched to a need without anyone holding a profile of it.

What is being broadcast
The payload changes. The mechanism does not. Case assignment, swarming, learning, mentoring and internal mobility all run on this one exchange.
This is the contract net protocol. Intelligent Swarming already uses it for cases. The same exchange serves every other personal matching problem in the building. One mechanic solving N problems.

Act three, the architecture

An append-only record cannot satisfy a deletion obligation by deleting, so it satisfies it by destroying the key instead.

Merge them and the strictest clock governs the whole record, so the engineer's history silently inherits a deletion schedule it should never have had.

Erasure destroys the key, not the file
The ciphertext remains and is permanently undecryptable, by anyone, including the engineer.
The discharge is itself auditable
A redacted event is appended recording when, under what legal basis, and on whose authority.
The chain still verifies
Nothing in the record was altered, so integrity checking still passes and tampering remains detectable.
Derived stores are rebuilt without it
Safe precisely because they were never authoritative. The solution path survives; the customer's content does not.
This is the answer to the obvious objection. A permanent worker-held record and a right to erasure are not in conflict, provided you separate the two data subjects before you need to.

  2. 2Why the layers cannot share a retention rule. Beyond the inherited clock, the case can no longer be traced once the payload expires, because the trace was stored inside the thing that expired.
  3. 3Schema drift. Genesis records are stamped with the schema in force on arrival and never migrated. Canonical projections are regenerated across the corpus when the schema moves, so a migration rebuilds a derived layer rather than rewriting history, and can be run again if it was wrong.
  4. 4Self-verifying reputation. Because the chain is verifiable and events are timestamped in sequence, an engineer can demonstrate that their record was neither fabricated nor back-dated. Once that record influences assignment and mobility, proving it was earned matters as much as having it.

Close, why anyone would build it

No turkey votes for Christmas. Engineers will invest in this because the Duo becomes the unit of work, and a Duo cannot be taken apart.

The pairing is what gets measured
Performance belongs to the Duo. At review time nobody attempts to separate what the engineer did from what their model did, because the work was done by the pair.
The method is what makes it personal
A generic model is the same on every desk. What lifts it is one engineer's accumulated way of working. That lives in the procedural store, encrypted to them.
Removing the engineer removes both
The model does not keep the method. It reverts to generic, and the organisation keeps the short bar rather than the long one.
And handover becomes possible
An engineer retires after thirty years and today there is no mechanism for the method to go anywhere. Because the store is theirs, they can hand all of it, model and method together, to a colleague, a team or the company, on the day they choose.
Nobody helps build the system that replaces them, so the architecture has to make the engineer harder to remove. This one does. The same lock that stops the method being taken is what lets it be given, and a gift counts as a gift because refusing was possible.

The limit of the claim on this slide. Losing the engineer is a real cost and a now visible one. It is not permanent irreplaceability.

Bainbridge said it in 1983, in five pages about power stations. Practice used to make perfect, and the exercises are evaporating: the 70 in 70-20-10 is being automated away, and with it the graded battery of cases that produced expertise as a by-product. Run the simulation and a career freezes at whatever title it had reached when the channel collapsed beneath it. Run it across a whole organisation and the pyramid hollows out: the bottom is cut back to the work that is left, people leave at the rate they always did, and the expert grades are never replenished. The evidence has caught up with her, in confidence effects on critical thinking and in payroll data showing entry level work vanishing where AI automates rather than augments. It matters because some skills are worth nothing in themselves and everything as foundations. The load-bearing kind is what we are quietly automating. So expertise has to be built on purpose, which turns a staffing apocalypse into a learning problem. KCS made knowledge a deliberate by-product of interaction but left expertise idiosyncratic and accidental, and what most organisations still call learning is just-in-case in a building where everything else became just-in-time. So break the course into modules whose objectives are capabilities with observable evidence, and make them addressable. The course survives this: from a catalogue of two hundred modules, one course is assembled for one engineer, guided by what they have actually done. Learning then runs in both directions, broadcast outward and requested inward. The aggregate of those anonymous requests forms a Learning Loop for people rather than an Evolve Loop for articles. Every one of those flows needs an answer to the same question: who is this for? A central register of what each person cannot yet do is an admission ticket to the next round of layoffs. So invert it: one register of four thousand becomes four thousand registers of one. That pairing of an engineer and their own model is the Support Duo, and the model is Alise. Only now does one mechanism serve everything: a broadcast goes out, every model bids locally with a single score, identity is revealed only on accept, and a case too big for one person goes to the two highest bids. That one mechanic solves case assignment, swarming, learning, mentoring and internal mobility. Alise curates cases from work otherwise out of reach, so learning continues after the channel has gone. Hold back a tenth of the automated volume to teach with and the expert grades keep being fed. Every case produces two things, the episodic record the company keeps and the procedural method the engineer keeps, because KCS captures the responder's knowledge but never their method. Only one scalar crosses the trust boundary, and since a feature vector is a profile, it never leaves either. Erasure destroys the key rather than the record, so a permanent worker-held history and a right to be forgotten can both hold at once. The same split makes it cheaper: the local model assembles context for free and only a curated prompt crosses the network. This is the safe version: co-determination came first, and the architecture serves the business as squarely as the person. The company takes the record, the resolution and the throughput, and the engineer walks out with their method intact. That is also why anyone would build it: the Duo becomes the unit of work, measured together and never split apart, so removing the engineer removes the method that made a generic model personal, and because the method is genuinely theirs, a retiring engineer can hand thirty years of it to a colleague on the day they choose. It is also a gap in European law that nobody in Brussels is addressing. Bainbridge's operators were left monitoring a system they could no longer run. The only question that matters is whether ours will still be able to tell when it is wrong.
